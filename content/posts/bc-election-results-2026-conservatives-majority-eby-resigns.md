> **The short version:** The BC election results are in: the B.C. Conservatives won a majority government on October 24, and interim leader Lorne Doerkson will become premier. David Eby, whose snap election call came two years early, resigned as NDP leader the same night. It is the first Conservative majority in British Columbia since 1928.

The BC election results delivered one of the sharpest political reversals in recent Canadian history. Five weeks ago the B.C. Conservatives were leaderless and losing MLAs. On Saturday night they swept to a majority, and the New Democrats, in power since 2017, were sent into Opposition. Below is what happened, how the seats broke down, who Lorne Doerkson is, what he has promised, and what comes next for the NDP and the province.

## BC election results at a glance

Counting continued overnight, so seat totals were still moving. [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12114060/bc-election-day-2026-polls-live-results/) showed the following projected seat count in the 93-seat legislature, where 47 seats are needed for a majority:

| Party | Leader | Projected seats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B.C. Conservatives | Lorne Doerkson (interim) | 64 |
| B.C. NDP | David Eby | 27 |
| B.C. Greens | Emily Lowan | 2 |
| CentreBC | Elenore Sturko | 0 |
| OneBC | Dallas Brodie (interim) | 0 |

Earlier counts were close to that. [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-conservatives-majority-government-9.7394961) had the Conservatives elected or leading in 62 ridings as of 10 p.m. PT, and [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/bc-s-left-wing-leader-loses-to-conservatives-in-vote-he-called) reported 63 Conservative, 28 NDP and two Green leads, adding that final results may take hours or days. Votes cast on election day are counted by hand, according to The Canadian Press.

Turnout in advance voting was heavy. Almost 900,000 people, or 24 per cent of registered voters, cast ballots in the advance polls, The Canadian Press reported via the [Toronto Star](https://www.thestar.com/news/canada/british-columbia/b-c-election-latest-voters-go-to-the-polls-to-decide-next-provincial-government/article_56382c84-cd47-5083-ab6d-a9c5a6dfa061.html).

### Where the Conservatives won

Much of the breakthrough came in the Lower Mainland, where CBC says the party stood to flip at least 10 seats from the NDP. The Conservatives were on track to sweep all of Surrey, with other pickups in Vancouver-Langara, Richmond-Steveston, Port Moody-Burquitlam and two seats in Coquitlam.

Other notable riding results reported by Global News and The Canadian Press:

- **Vancouver-Point Grey:** Eby held his own seat, in what CTV described as a close contest with his Conservative challenger.
- **Saanich North and the Islands:** Green Leader Emily Lowan won in her first election.
- **Vancouver-Quilchena:** OneBC interim leader Dallas Brodie lost to Conservative Carson Binda.
- **Surrey South:** CentreBC Leader Elenore Sturko lost her former seat to Conservative Megan Knight.
- **Peace River North:** Independent MLA Jordan Kealy lost to Conservative Curtis Whitford.
- **Vancouver-Renfrew:** NDP MLA Adrian Dix was re-elected.

## Why David Eby's snap election backfired

Eby called the election on September 22, a full two years before the next scheduled vote, while holding a one-seat majority in the legislature, [CityNews Vancouver](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/10/24/bc-conservatives-lorne-doerkson-win-election/) reports. He first said he needed a stronger mandate to deal with economic uncertainty brought on by Donald Trump and U.S. tariffs, then said the vote would end the frustrations of a "stalled out" legislature. Opponents saw it as an opportunistic move timed to Conservative turmoil.

That turmoil vanished almost overnight. Kerry-Lynne Findlay quit as Conservative leader on September 20 amid a caucus revolt, two days before the election call. CBC reports that the snap vote "catalyzed" the party: several MLAs who had left the caucus returned after Doerkson was appointed interim leader. Critics also hammered the timing, so close to B.C.'s municipal elections.

Political scientist Hamish Telford told CityNews the NDP campaign "was predicated on Kerry-Lynne Findlay being the leader of the Conservative Party." He added: "When she resigned, really he (Eby) shouldn't have gone through with the election. He should have called it off on the grounds that the opposition didn't have a leader. But he put the trains in motion and he couldn't stop them."

Stewart Prest, another political scientist, was just as blunt to CTV News: "This was an avoidable election. He didn't need to do this for another two years. And indeed, nobody in the province wanted him to do this."

Green Leader Emily Lowan put it this way on election night, per Global News: "David Eby called this snap election two years early for nobody but himself."

## Who is Lorne Doerkson, B.C.'s premier-designate?

Doerkson went from MLA to board-appointed party leader to premier-elect in less than five weeks, CityNews notes. Here is what the reporting says about him:

- MLA for Cariboo-Chilcotin since 2020.
- A former publisher of the local newspaper and a former rodeo rider.
- One of the first defectors from BC United to the Conservatives as that party imploded, joining the Conservatives in 2024.
- Recommended as leader by the remaining Conservative caucus on September 21, 2026. He hurried back from a trip to Africa and was campaigning to become premier two days later.

He leaned on the rodeo story in his victory speech. "I have watched a lot of people get thrown at a rodeo, and I'll say I've been thrown a few times myself," he said. "But what I've learned from that is that you pick yourself up, dust yourself off, and you get back on."

His message to rural voters drew some of the loudest cheers. "For a very long time, a lot of British Columbians have felt like Victoria forgot about them, like the map maybe stopped at Hope, but tonight the whole map counts," he said, as quoted by CBC.

### A party rebuilt in six years

The scale of the comeback is hard to overstate. CityNews reports the Conservatives won less than two per cent of the vote in the 2020 provincial election. Under John Rustad, a former B.C. Liberal who became leader in 2023, they won 44 seats in October 2024, three short of government. Rustad was forced out in December 2025, interim leader Trevor Halford followed, and Findlay lasted 113 days before quitting. CBC notes this is the first time since 1928 that a party running under the Conservative banner has formed a majority in B.C.

## What the Conservatives promised

Doerkson ran on an economy-first platform. Based on reporting from CBC, Global News and The Canadian Press, the main commitments include:

| Area | Promise |
| --- | --- |
| Taxes | A tax cut and tax freeze. "A $1,000 tax cut is just the start, with no new taxes," Doerkson said. |
| Energy | Doubling natural gas production by 2032 (CBC); The Canadian Press reported a pledge to triple LNG production by 2035. |
| Resources | Faster permitting and mining exploration. "We will cut red tape like it's never been cut before." |
| Budget | Shrink the deficit to $10.8 billion by 2030 (CityNews). |
| Infrastructure | A 10-lane bridge to replace the George Massey Tunnel, SkyTrain to Newton, and a new women and children's hospital in Surrey. |
| Health | "Health care that is fully and publicly funded," with rural ERs and maternity wards kept open. |
| Social policy | Repeal the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act (DRIPA), end the SOGI 123 school resource, and crack down on public drug use while prioritizing treatment. |

Several of those, especially repealing DRIPA, are likely to become early flashpoints with First Nations and the new Opposition.

Prime Minister Mark Carney congratulated Doerkson on social media and said he looks forward to working with him "to get more homes built, unleash our full potential as an energy superpower, and build a stronger British Columbia and a stronger Canada for all."

## Eby resigns: what's next for the B.C. NDP

Eby conceded, called Doerkson to wish him well, and announced he would step down as party leader. "This is obviously not the result that any of us wanted," he told supporters, according to [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/i-accept-full-responsibility-eby-steps-down-as-bc-ndp-leader/). "A number of my colleagues, some of the hardest working MLAs I know for their communities, were not elected tonight. It breaks my heart, and I accept full responsibility for that outcome."

He also spoke about the personal toll: "My family has paid a very significant cost for this work, and I am keen to spend some time with my kids, with my wife, with our friends, and not worry if someone is videotaping me while I am singing karaoke."

Re-elected MLA Adrian Dix called it "one of the most disappointing nights in the modern history" of the party, The Canadian Press reported. No timeline for an NDP leadership race had been announced as of this writing.

## Why the BC election matters beyond B.C.

British Columbia is Canada's third most populous province, and Bloomberg framed the result as a swing to the political right. It also comes as provinces and Ottawa grapple with U.S. trade pressure. Eby raised it in his concession remarks: "Donald Trump is attacking our workers. He's targeting our businesses and he is threatening our sovereignty," he said on election night, per Global News.

## What to watch next

- **Final seat count:** Hand counts and any close ridings could shift totals by a seat or two over the coming days.
- **Swearing-in and cabinet:** Doerkson's first cabinet will show how quickly he moves on taxes, energy and DRIPA. A date had not been announced at the time of writing.
- **NDP leadership:** Watch for the party to name an interim leader and set rules for a leadership race.
- **Ottawa and B.C.:** Carney's emphasis on energy and housing suggests early talks on natural gas and LNG projects.
- **First Nations response:** The DRIPA repeal pledge is the promise most likely to face legal and political pushback.
