> **The short version:** Daylight saving time ends at 2 a.m. on Sunday, November 1, 2026, when clocks go back one hour. It is the earliest date the federal rule allows, it falls on Halloween weekend, and despite a House vote this summer to make daylight saving time permanent, the Senate has not acted, so this fall back is happening as usual.

Daylight saving time is about to end for the year, and with the change arriving a week earlier than many people expect, "when do clocks fall back" is one of the fastest-rising searches in the U.S. this weekend. Here is the exact timing, why 2026 is unusual, what it means for your evenings, and the latest on the long-running push to stop changing the clocks.

## When daylight saving time ends in 2026

According to [The Old Farmer's Almanac](https://www.almanac.com/content/when-daylight-saving-time), daylight saving time ends on **Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 a.m. local time**. At that moment clocks move back to 1:00 a.m., which means:

- You gain one hour of sleep on Saturday night into Sunday.
- The 1 a.m. hour happens twice that night.
- Sunrise and sunset both move about an hour earlier on the clock starting Sunday.

Smartphones, computers and most connected devices switch automatically. Clocks you will probably need to change by hand include the ones on your oven, microwave, car dashboard, older alarm clocks and some thermostats.

## Why the time change is so early this year

If it feels like the clocks are changing sooner than usual, you are right. Under the Uniform Time Act, daylight saving time begins on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. As [The Columbus Dispatch](https://www.dispatch.com/story/news/2026/10/24/time-change-clocks-fall-back-weekend-date-daylight-savings-time-2026/92398559007/) explains, November 1 falls on a Sunday in 2026, so the change lands on the earliest date the rule allows.

That timing creates a few quirks:

- **Halloween night gets an extra hour.** Florida Today points out the change falls on Halloween weekend, so Saturday night parties technically run an hour longer.
- **The World Series could be affected.** A potential Game 7 of the [2026 World Series](/blog/world-series-2026-brewers-rays-schedule-how-to-watch) is scheduled for Saturday, October 31, the night before clocks change.
- **It comes just before Election Day.** Polls open on November 3 under standard time, so early risers will see daylight sooner that morning. Our [2026 midterm elections guide](/blog/2026-midterm-elections-early-voting-what-to-know) has voting deadlines.

The biggest everyday change is darker evenings. In Ohio, for example, the Dispatch reports that on the first day of standard time:

In California, [The Desert Sun](https://www.desertsun.com/story/news/nation/california/2026/10/19/will-california-turn-their-clocks-back-this-year-heres-what-to-know/92333336007/) reports residents will also turn clocks back on November 1, trading an extra hour of sleep for earlier sunsets through the winter.

### Tips for an easier switch

Gaining an hour is gentler than losing one, but it still shifts your routine:

1. **Move bedtime gradually.** Going to bed 15 minutes later each night for a few days before the change helps your body adjust.
2. **Get morning light.** Brighter mornings after the switch can help reset your internal clock.
3. **Plan for darker commutes.** Drivers and pedestrians should expect evening rush hour in low light starting the week of November 2.
4. **Use the change as a reminder.** The twice-a-year clock change is an easy prompt for household checks, like testing smoke and carbon monoxide detectors.

### Shift workers and overnight schedules

Because the 1 a.m. hour repeats, anyone working an overnight shift on October 31 into November 1 will work an extra hour by the clock. Hospitals, transit systems and other 24-hour operations typically plan for this in advance, but if you are paid hourly and work that night, it is worth checking how your employer records the repeated hour. The same applies to medication schedules: if you take a dose at a fixed time overnight, decide in advance whether you will follow the old or the new clock.

## Is this the last time we fall back?

Probably not, at least not yet. The effort to stop changing clocks has a long history:

- The **Sunshine Protection Act**, which would make daylight saving time permanent, was first introduced in March 2018 by then-Sen. Marco Rubio and Rep. Vern Buchanan, Florida Today reports. Versions followed in 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2025, when Sen. Rick Scott introduced the latest Senate bill.
- On **July 14, 2026**, the House passed the Sunshine Protection Act in a bipartisan **308-117** vote, according to the Columbus Dispatch.
- The bill is now pending before the **Senate Commerce, Science and Transportation Committee**, the Dispatch reports.
- Neither chamber is scheduled to be in session before the November 1 change, and Florida Today reports lawmakers will not return until November 9.

So for 2026, you will still fall back. Whether you spring forward in March and then stay there depends on Senate action in the coming months.

### Why states can't just stop on their own

Federal law lets states opt out of daylight saving time and stay on standard time all year, but it does not let them stay on daylight saving time permanently. That is why Florida, where lawmakers have pushed for year-round daylight time for years, still falls back every November, as Florida Today explains. Any switch to permanent daylight time needs Congress.

## Where clocks don't change

Not everyone in the U.S. changes their clocks. According to Florida Today:

- **Arizona** and **Hawaii** stay on standard time all year.
- **U.S. territories** including Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands do not observe daylight saving time.

If you have family, colleagues or meetings in those places, the time difference between you will shift by an hour on November 1.

### Daylight saving time pros and cons

The debate usually comes down to which hours of daylight people value more:

| Argument | Permanent daylight time | Permanent standard time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evenings | More light after work and school | Darker winter evenings |
| Mornings | Very dark winter mornings, especially in northern and western parts of time zones | More morning light |
| Status | Passed the House in July 2026, pending in the Senate | Already allowed by federal law; Arizona and Hawaii use it |

Florida Today notes that supporters of ending the clock changes point to brighter afternoons and more economic activity. Critics of permanent daylight time often cite dark winter mornings for students and commuters.
