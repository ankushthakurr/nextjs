> **The short version:** The California governor race is in its final week, and Democrat Xavier Becerra holds a double-digit lead over Republican Steve Hilton in every recent public poll. The winner succeeds Gavin Newsom. Election Day is November 3, and mailed ballots only need to be postmarked by that date.

The California governor race once looked like one of the most unpredictable contests in the country. Early this year, Republicans Steve Hilton and Riverside County Sheriff Chad Bianco topped some polls while Xavier Becerra sat in single digits, [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/poll-gives-becerra-27-point-lead-over-hilton-in-california-governor-race) reported. Then the field reshuffled, Becerra surged, and the two men finished first and second in the June primary. Six days before the election, the race to replace Newsom has settled into a clear pattern. Below are the latest polls, how the candidates differ on the issues voters care most about, how much money each has raised, and everything you need to know to vote and follow the results.

## California governor race: the latest polls

The most recent independent survey, from [Emerson College Polling](https://emersoncollegepolling.com/california-2026-poll-xavier-becerra-holds-27-point-lead-over-steve-hilton/) and Inside California Politics, was conducted October 17 to 19 among 1,000 likely voters, according to [KTLA](https://ktla.com/news/politics/inside-california-politics/governors-race-poll-becerra-leads-hilton/). It found Becerra ahead by 27 points. A second poll, by McLaughlin & Associates for the California Post, showed a smaller but still large gap, the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/10/24/us-news/xavier-becerra-leads-steve-hilton-in-california-governor-poll-despite-widespread-voter-dissatisfaction/) reported.

| Poll | Becerra (D) | Hilton (R) | Margin | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emerson College, Oct. 17-19 | 60% | 33% | Becerra +27 | 7% undecided; with leaners, 64% to 36% |
| McLaughlin & Associates for California Post | 55% | 39.3% | Becerra +15.7 | Independents split 46% to 44% for Becerra |
| Earlier surveys cited by Courthouse News | | | +18 (August), +25 (late September) | Shows the lead growing over the fall |

"California independents break for Becerra by 10 points, 46% to 36%, a group Hilton would need more support among to compete in the general election," Spencer Kimball, executive director of Emerson College Polling, said in the poll release.

The two polls disagree on how independents are leaning, which is the main reason their margins differ. Both agree on the direction of the race. The McLaughlin poll also found the candidates evenly matched on image: Becerra's favorable and unfavorable ratings were both 42%, while Hilton stood at 39% favorable and 42% unfavorable.

### What voters say matters most

Emerson asked likely voters to name their top issue. The economy led at 45%, followed by housing affordability at 17%, with healthcare and immigration at 10% each. Asked who is responsible for the state of California's economy, 44% named President Trump, 38% named Governor Newsom, 7% named the Republican majority in Congress, and 8% named someone else. Newsom's own job approval stood at 46%, with 43% disapproving.

That split helps explain the strategies on both sides. Hilton is running against "16 years of one-party rule" in Sacramento, while Becerra has spent much of the campaign tying his opponent to the president.

## Who are Xavier Becerra and Steve Hilton?

**Xavier Becerra (Democrat)** is a former congressman, a former California attorney general and the U.S. Health and Human Services secretary under President Joe Biden. He is the son of Mexican immigrants. He won the June 2 top-two primary with 28% of the vote, [KTLA](https://ktla.com/news/politics/inside-california-politics/governors-race-poll-becerra-leads-hilton/) reported. "I am different from every governor that we've ever seen because no one brings the experience or the credentials that I bring, and no one brings the life experience," Becerra said, according to [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/10/california-governor-race-final-stretch/).

**Steve Hilton (Republican)** is a former Fox News host and a former adviser to British Prime Minister David Cameron. He is a British immigrant who moved to California in 2012 with his wife, a Silicon Valley executive, and this is his first run for office in the state. He finished second in the primary with 24.6%. Donald Trump endorsed him during the primary, though Hilton has since tried to put some distance between himself and the president and the Republican Party, CalMatters reports.

How they got here matters. Former Democratic Rep. Eric Swalwell was running neck and neck with Hilton and Bianco early in the year before leaving the race in April, which reshaped the Democratic side. Becerra then rose quickly and won the top spot in June.

## Where they stand on the issues

The candidates' only scheduled debate took place on September 30 at CNN's studio in Burbank, moderated by Jake Tapper and Dana Bash. [CalMatters' debate recap](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/09/california-general-election-gubernatorial-debate-cnn/) and its final-stretch coverage outline the main differences:

| Issue | Xavier Becerra | Steve Hilton |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost of living and energy | Two hours of free electricity during off-peak daytime hours for certain low-income households | $3 gas and steep tax cuts across all income levels |
| Housing | Cut bureaucratic hurdles for housing approvals, expand down payment assistance, stop Wall Street investors from competing with would-be homeowners | Build 10 new cities and allow more construction on undeveloped land |
| Homelessness | Prevention first; clear encampments while offering shelter, mental health care and addiction treatment | Up to 30 large recovery centers; end Housing First and harm reduction funding |
| Healthcare | Keep covering people who would lose Medi-Cal under federal cuts of up to $30 billion a year, by cutting wasteful and administrative spending | No detailed plan in the coverage we reviewed |
| Immigration | Sue federal immigration agencies over aggressive enforcement | Has praised Trump's deportation campaign; says he wants to "turn down the temperature" on the debate |
| Artificial intelligence | Limits on AI data centers in communities that oppose them | Would wait to assess whether tougher AI rules are needed |

Hilton has also promised an "Office of Fun and Freedom" that would loosen nightlife rules and allow dogs in more state parks, and a requirement that animal shelters notify the public before euthanizing dogs.

### Homelessness: the sharpest contrast

In interviews with [ABC7 San Francisco](https://abc7news.com/post/election-2026-heres-where-california-gubernatorial-candidates-steve-hilton-xavier-becerra-stand-housing-homelessness/19966958/), Hilton said he aims to effectively end mass homelessness within his first year. Asked whether that is realistic, he said, "Yes, it is. It's not just possible. It's essential." His plan calls for as many as 30 recovery centers holding about 5,000 people each, or temporary shelter for roughly 150,000 people. "We're not talking about building apartment buildings," he said.

Becerra emphasized prevention and accountability for service providers. "We have to do everything we can to prevent them from falling into homelessness," he told ABC7. "If you're helping address homelessness, then we'll scale you up. But if you got money and you didn't do anything, we'll have to close you down."

### Trump as the main character

At the debate, Becerra warned, "You're going to end up with Trump results if you follow Steve Hilton's plans." Hilton shot back: "All you ever say is 'Trump, Trump, Trump.' And that's nothing but an insult to every Californian who is desperate for something to change in this state."

On gas prices, which jumped after the February U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran according to Courthouse News, Becerra said, "The first thing, obviously, we have to do is get Trump to end his crazy war abroad. But what we can do in California is make sure we don't allow the spikes to come because of anything we do in California."

## Money and campaign tactics in the final stretch

Becerra has raised $40 million and Hilton $33.6 million, [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/10/california-governor-race-final-stretch/) reports. Hilton's total is about double what the leading Republican raised in the failed 2021 recall of Newsom.

Hilton has run an unconventional campaign: barnstorming the state, flooding social media with provocative ads, using AI to portray himself as a gladiator, and texting selfie videos from Los Angeles' Skid Row. His "Just try it" ad series, aimed at liberal voters, drew a backlash from conservatives. He also accused Becerra of lying about his parents' immigrant backgrounds, which Latino critics said misunderstood the Mexican American experience.

Becerra kept a lower profile over the summer, then shifted to campaigning against Proposition 39 and helping Democrats in competitive House districts in Southern California and the Central Valley, including a Bakersfield rally for Randy Villegas in the 22nd District.

Even GOP strategists credit Hilton's effort while doubting the outcome. "He's run a good race," Republican consultant Tim Rosales told CalMatters. "The fact is that Democratic voters in California, which there are a lot of them, can agree with Hilton on the issues. They just want a Democrat to try to solve them."

## How to vote in California by November 3

The governor's race tops a long ballot. Californians will also pick a lieutenant governor, insurance commissioner and other statewide officers, half the state Senate, every Assembly seat, and 14 ballot propositions, including Proposition 39, which would require photo ID to vote in person, Courthouse News reports. For the national picture, see our guide to the [2026 midterm elections and early voting](/blog/2026-midterm-elections-early-voting-what-to-know).

Key rules from the [California Secretary of State](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/upcoming-elections/general-election-november-3-2026) and county officials:

- **Election Day:** Tuesday, November 3. Polls and vote centers are open 7 a.m. to 8 p.m., and anyone in line by 8 p.m. can vote.
- **Mail ballots:** Must be postmarked on or before November 3. The state suggests mailing at least seven days early. If you mail on Election Day, ask a postal employee for a manual postmark.
- **Late arrivals:** Ballots postmarked by Election Day count if they arrive by November 10, [NBC San Diego](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/elections/san-diego-voting-guide-how-to-vote-register-dates-2026-midterm/4092180/) reports.
- **Missed the registration deadline?** Online registration closed October 19, but same-day registration is available through Election Day at county elections offices and in-person voting locations.
- **Track your ballot:** Sign up for the state's "Where's My Ballot?" service on the Secretary of State's site.

## When will we know who won?

County election officials begin posting results at 8 p.m. on November 3 and keep updating throughout the night and in the days and weeks that follow, because California counts a large share of mail ballots that arrive after Election Day. Officials have until December 3 to certify results.

The winner becomes California's 41st governor and takes over from Newsom. Whoever wins will inherit the questions that dominated this campaign: housing costs, homelessness, gas prices, and how hard to push back on the Trump administration.

## What to watch next

- **Final polls this week.** Watch whether Hilton narrows the gap with independents, the group both pollsters say he needs.
- **Proposition 39.** The voter ID measure has drawn the most attention of any measure and is a focus of Becerra's closing campaign.
- **House races.** Becerra's stops in the Central Valley and Southern California point to the districts that could decide control of Congress.
- **Turnout and the count.** Mail ballots arriving through November 10 can shift margins after election night.
