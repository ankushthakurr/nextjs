> **The short version:** The Trump mortgage executive order signed on October 22 targets three costs borrowers know well: private mortgage insurance, title insurance on refinances, and rates on a small set of government loans. It also creates a task force on factory-built homes. The changes are real but narrow, mostly depend on decisions by the Federal Housing Finance Agency, and will not touch today's 7.3% mortgage rates.

The Trump mortgage executive order has become one of the most searched housing stories in the US this week, as buyers and homeowners try to work out whether it will actually lower their monthly payment. Titled "Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People," the order was signed on Thursday, October 22, 2026, less than two weeks before the November midterm elections. Below is what the order says, line by line where it matters, who stands to benefit, what experts and industry groups are saying, and what it will not do.

## What the Trump mortgage executive order does

The [full text on WhiteHouse.gov](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/10/decreasing-housing-costs-for-the-american-people/) has four working sections. In plain terms:

| Section | What it directs | Who acts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sec. 2: Mortgage insurance | A pilot that uses automated home valuations to find borrowers who qualify to drop mortgage insurance, and cancels it proactively | FHFA, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac |
| Sec. 3: Manufactured housing | A new Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation with 180 days to send recommendations | HUD, FHFA, USDA, Commerce, VA |
| Sec. 4: Title insurance | Turn the title acceptance pilot into an ongoing nationwide program for eligible refinances | FHFA, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac |
| Sec. 5: Direct loan rates | Reduce interest rates on single-family loans made directly by the two departments | USDA, VA |

The order's stated purpose is to "reduce regulatory barriers to mortgage origination and costs impeding homeownership" and to embrace automated "risk and value assessment technologies." Like most executive orders, it must be carried out "consistent with applicable law and subject to the availability of appropriations," and it does not create any right a borrower can enforce in court.

## PMI cancellation: who benefits and when

Private mortgage insurance, or PMI, protects the lender when a borrower puts down less than 20%. It is one of the biggest line items the order targets, and it is common. Citing Urban Institute data, [Yahoo Finance reports](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/trumps-housing-affordability-order-targets-costs-that-hundreds-of-thousands-of-homeowners-pay-each-year-184228025.html) that 36% of conventional mortgage borrowers paid PMI in 2024, up from 25.4% in 2016.

### What PMI costs

- CNBC puts the annual cost of PMI at roughly 0.2% to 2% of the loan amount.
- Freddie Mac estimates premiums of $30 to $70 a month for every $100,000 borrowed, according to Yahoo Finance. On a $300,000 loan, that works out to about $90 to $210 a month.
- The trade group U.S. Mortgage Insurers says premiums are down 25% since 2017, per Yahoo Finance.

### How the new pilot would work

Under Section 2, the FHFA Director may direct Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to periodically review the loans they own or guarantee, use automated valuation models to estimate each home's current market value, and cancel mortgage insurance "proactively" when the borrower meets the equity, payment-history and other requirements. Servicers would be told to notify borrowers promptly and to cut unnecessary documentation requests and appraisal costs.

The key detail is in the fine print: the pilot is "applicable only to mortgages made after the establishment of the pilot program." If you already have a loan with PMI, this order does not cancel it for you.

### What existing borrowers can do today

Current rules already give conventional borrowers a path out. As [CNBC explains](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/trump-mortgage-executive-order-costs.html), you can generally ask your servicer to remove PMI once your balance falls below 80% of the home's original value, and the servicer may remove it automatically at 78%. If your home has gained value, asking your servicer about a new valuation is still the fastest route. FHA loans are different: CNBC notes they require mortgage insurance on all loans, and the order's PMI pilot covers Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans only.

## Cheaper refinances without lender's title insurance

Section 4 goes after closing costs on refinances. Title and title-insurance fees generally make up the largest share of mortgage closing costs, according to CNBC. FHFA already runs a title acceptance pilot that lets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac accept an automated title review instead of a lender's title insurance policy on some refinances.

The order asks the FHFA Director to expand that pilot into an ongoing program available nationwide through both companies. The pilot was originally planned to end in November 2027, CNBC reports, and borrowers could save $500 to $1,500 in closing costs on loans sold under it.

In expanding the program, the Director is told to consider:

- Allowing refinances with loan-to-value ratios above the current 80% limit where title risk stays low.
- Removing geographic limits and certifying more title-review providers to add competition.
- Waiving appraisals, income re-verification and other paperwork for limited cash-out refinances that lower the borrower's monthly payment.
- Reducing or eliminating loan-level price adjustments and other fees on those refinances.

The order also requires that savings be passed through to borrowers and that lenders tell borrowers about them.

Some voices urge caution. Realtor.com quoted Chris Morton saying efforts to improve affordability are "critical" but that "fraud is unaffordable, and the threats facing homeowners are growing," adding that lower costs should not "shift significant financial risks onto American families."

## VA and USDA loans, and the manufactured housing task force

Section 5 directs the Secretaries of Agriculture and Veterans Affairs to reduce interest rates on single-family home loans their departments make directly. Yahoo Finance describes these direct loans as a small number of home loans, so few borrowers will feel this change. The order gives no target rate.

Section 3 sets up the Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation, co-chaired by the HUD Secretary and the FHFA Director, and including the Secretaries of Agriculture, Commerce and Veterans Affairs. Within 180 days it must recommend ways to standardize approvals and inspections across federal programs, remove duplicate reviews that slow factory production, and use federal financing to encourage more affordable factory-built homes. It will also look at whether federal grants can encourage states and cities to adopt standardized permitting.

HUD Secretary Scott Turner said in a statement quoted by [Realtor.com](https://www.realtor.com/news/real-estate-news/trump-executive-order-housing-afforability-manufactured-october-2026/), "For decades, home building has been crippled by regulatory red tape, but the Task Force on Manufactured Housing Innovation can usher in the next generation of housing by modernizing standards and speeding up approvals across government."

## What experts and the industry are saying

FHFA Director Bill Pulte called it "the greatest Housing Executive Order in History" in a post on X, according to Realtor.com, and was quoted by CNBC as saying "millions of Americans will save thousands and thousands of dollars."

Others are more measured. Keith Gumbinger, vice president of mortgage site HSH, told CNBC that "most mortgages ... are carrying PMI at this point in time," but added: "I don't know that it's necessarily a game-changer in terms of the cost of housing, which is dictated by home price and mortgage rates at the moment. But you know, every little bit certainly helps."

Seth Appleton, president of U.S. Mortgage Insurers, said, "We commend the President's focus on lowering costs for future homeowners, and the private mortgage insurance industry is proud to already be delivering on that goal."

Bob Broeksmit of the Mortgage Bankers Association said lenders "welcome these latest initiatives to reduce the costs of refinancing and expand innovative manufactured housing options," per Realtor.com.

Investors were watching too: Yahoo Finance's report page showed shares of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac each down about 5% on Friday.

## Why it is happening now

Housing costs have become a political problem heading into the November midterms, as [Reuters notes](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-signs-executive-order-aimed-reducing-housing-costs-2026-10-22/). The market backdrop is weak:

- The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.32% this week, down slightly from 7.40% the week before, according to Reuters.
- Contracts to buy previously owned homes fell to a record low in September.
- Single-family housing starts fell 4.6% in September, and permits for future construction dropped 4% to their lowest level in three and a half years.

None of the order's measures change the rate a typical buyer is quoted today. As Gumbinger put it, home prices and mortgage rates drive affordability, and those are outside this order's reach. ## What to watch next

- **FHFA's decisions.** Almost every mortgage change depends on the FHFA Director deciding it serves Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and the public. Watch for formal pilot announcements and start dates.
- **Eligibility rules for the PMI pilot.** Equity thresholds, payment-history requirements and which valuation models count will decide how many future borrowers actually benefit.
- **The title program rollout.** Whether the 80% loan-to-value cap is raised and new states are added will determine how many refinancers can skip lender's title insurance.
- **VA and USDA rate notices.** Look for the new direct-loan rates and when they apply.
- **The task force report.** Recommendations on manufactured housing are due within 180 days, which points to around April 2027.
- **Mortgage rates.** Weekly Freddie Mac data will matter far more for most budgets than anything in the order.
