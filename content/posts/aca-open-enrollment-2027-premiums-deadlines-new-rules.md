> **The short version:** ACA open enrollment for 2027 coverage opens November 1 and runs to January 15 on HealthCare.gov. Proposed premiums are up about 15% on average before subsidies, the extra pandemic-era subsidies are gone, new identity checks apply when you use a broker, and at least a dozen insurers are leaving some markets. Shopping, not auto-renewing, is the best defense.

ACA open enrollment starts in one week, and for Americans who buy their own health insurance, this year's shopping season comes with more moving parts than usual. Prices are rising again, fewer people qualify for generous help, the federal marketplace is adding fraud checks, and some insurers are pulling out entirely. Here is what changed and what to do before the deadlines.

## ACA open enrollment 2027 dates and deadlines

According to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/aca-open-enrollment-premiums-rules-brokers/) and Medical Daily, the HealthCare.gov schedule is:

| Deadline | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Nov. 1, 2026** | Open enrollment opens in most states |
| **Dec. 15, 2026** | Pick a plan by this date for coverage starting Jan. 1 |
| **Dec. 16, 2026** | If you did nothing, you may see a message that you were automatically re-enrolled |
| **Jan. 15, 2027** | Last day to enroll or switch, for coverage starting Feb. 1 |

Some state-run exchanges set their own dates. CBS News notes that Idaho, Connecticut and Massachusetts start earlier, so check your state's marketplace if you do not use HealthCare.gov.

## Premiums: how much prices are rising

Healthinsurance.org analyzed proposed 2027 rate filings and found a **median and weighted average increase of about 15% before subsidies**. CBS News reports that comes after an estimated 26% increase last year, so this is a second straight year of double-digit hikes.

The range across states is wide:

| Lowest proposed increases | Highest proposed increases |
| --- | --- |
| Vermont 3% | Arizona 27.9% |
| Iowa 5.7% | Alabama 25% |
| New York 6% | New Mexico 24.4% |
| Utah 6.7% | Kansas 24.2% |
| Arkansas 7.6% | Alaska 22.9% |

*Source: [healthinsurance.org](https://www.healthinsurance.org/blog/health-insurance-premium-increases-for-2027-proposed-rates-by-state-and-what-consumers-should-know/) proposed average rate changes. Final approved rates can differ.*

Large states land in the middle: California 9.9%, Florida 10.4%, Texas 13.1%, Pennsylvania 16% and Georgia 20.7%.

### Why premiums keep climbing

Healthinsurance.org lists several drivers:

- **Higher medical costs**, including hospital prices, drug prices such as GLP-1 weight loss and diabetes medicines, labor costs and more use of care.
- **General inflation.**
- **A sicker risk pool.** When the enhanced subsidies expired, healthier people were more likely to drop coverage, leaving insurers with costlier members.
- **Federal rule changes**, including the 2025 Marketplace Integrity rule, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and the 2027 payment notice, which insurers expect to reduce enrollment.

Out-of-pocket limits are rising too. CBS News reports the maximum out-of-pocket cap will be **$12,000 for an individual and $24,000 for a family** in 2027, up about 13% from $10,600 and $21,200 this year.

## Subsidies are smaller after the enhancements expired

The enhanced premium tax credits that made coverage cheaper during and after the pandemic expired at the end of 2025. CBS News reports that change led millions to drop or lose coverage in the first months of 2026.

For 2027:

- **Most buyers still get a subsidy**, but it is less generous than before.
- **Help stops above 400% of the federal poverty level.** CBS News gives examples: about $63,840 for a single person, $86,560 for a couple and $109,280 for a family of three.
- **Many lawfully present immigrants lose eligibility** under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, including H-1B visa holders, refugees, asylees, people with temporary protected status and those with pending green card applications. Green card holders, certain Cuban and Haitian immigrants, and citizens of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands and Palau remain eligible, CBS News reports.

Despite the higher sticker prices, Louise Norris told CBS News that "for most folks, overall affordability will stay about the same," adding that if you managed this year's premiums, next year "should be similar." Anne Petry warned that "the biggest issue we will face is affordability" and that "there definitely will come a breaking point for people."

## New identity checks and fewer brokers

The federal marketplace is tightening rules aimed at fraud, after the administration canceled policies covering about 760,000 enrollees suspected of improper sign-ups in August, according to CBS News.

What changes for you:

1. **Agent authorization.** Anyone using an agent or broker on HealthCare.gov must approve that specific agent with a one-time code or link sent by text, email or automated call.
2. **Extra ID proofing for "high-risk" applicants.** That includes first-time applicants, people moving states, people switching to a new agent, and anyone with a monthly premium of $30 or less. They must verify identity through a CMS-approved service such as Experian, or upload a driver's license, passport or immigration papers.
3. **A moratorium on new brokers.** Agents who were not registered with the federal marketplace in 2026 cannot help with 2027 enrollments until February at the earliest. Two broker groups have sued over the moratorium, CBS News reports.

Joshua Brooker told CBS News that "up to 1 in 5 consumers is going to have to do extra identity proofing." Stacey Pogue cautioned that some legitimate people may struggle "because they don't have a credit file, or have moved, or have changed names," and advised: "If you rely on an agent or broker... I would reach out early."

## Is your insurer leaving? Check before Dec. 15

[Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/aca-insurer-exits-2027-automatic-reassignment-480376) counts at least a dozen insurers leaving some ACA marketplaces after 2026, including:

- **Cigna:** all 11 of its marketplace states (Arizona, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, Tennessee, Texas and Virginia), about 369,000 enrollees
- **Baylor Scott & White Health Plan:** Texas, about 100,000 enrollees
- **CareSource:** Indiana, Ohio and West Virginia
- **Molina:** Illinois, Ohio, Mississippi and Kentucky, shrinking from 14 states to six
- **Medica:** Iowa, Kansas and Oklahoma
- **PacificSource:** Idaho, Montana and Oregon
- **UnitedHealthcare:** Georgia
- **Providence Health Plan:** Oregon

If your insurer has no similar plan next year, HealthCare.gov can automatically move you into a comparable plan from another company. Medical Daily stresses that the new plan **is not guaranteed to include the same doctors, hospitals or prescriptions**. Rules vary by state; in Connecticut, ConnectiCare enrollees must pick a new plan themselves to keep marketplace coverage.

## Your open enrollment checklist

Pulling together advice from Medical Daily, healthinsurance.org and the experts quoted by CBS News:

- **Read every letter** from your insurer and the marketplace.
- **Update your 2027 income estimate**, since subsidies depend on it.
- **Look up each doctor, hospital and prescription** in any plan you consider.
- **Compare total yearly cost**, including the deductible and out-of-pocket maximum, not just the monthly premium.
- **Don't just auto-renew.** "If you just sit on the sidelines and let the policy auto-renew, you might miss out on what could be a better opportunity," Norris told CBS News.
- **Book broker help early** if you use one.
- **Have ID ready** in case you are asked to verify.
