> **The short version:** Thanksgiving travel in 2026 runs into a coastal storm that brings heavy rain and strong winds to the East Coast on Wednesday night and Thanksgiving Day, plus rain, king tides and mountain snow out West. It follows weeks of airline tech failures, so build in extra time, leave in the morning if you are driving, and know your refund rights before you get to the airport.

Thanksgiving travel this year was always going to be crowded. AAA expects 81.5 million Americans to head at least 50 miles from home over the holiday period, and the FAA is planning for its busiest Thanksgiving in the air in 16 years. Now forecasters are tracking storms on both coasts, and travelers are still uneasy after an FAA network failure and outages at United and Delta in recent weeks. Here is what the forecast looks like day by day, the best and worst times to hit the road, which airlines are already offering free changes, and what you are owed if your flight falls apart.

## How many people are making the Thanksgiving trip

AAA's [Thanksgiving forecast](https://newsroom.aaa.com/2026/11/aaa-thanksgiving-holiday-travel-forecast/) covers Tuesday, November 24 through Monday, November 30. It projects 81.5 million travelers, which is slightly lower than the 81.6 million who traveled last year. AAA calls it a 0.1% decline, or about 121,000 fewer people.

"After three years of record highs, the number of travelers is slightly lower partly because of higher travel costs this year," said Stacey Barber, vice president of AAA Travel. "But travelers won't notice much of a difference on the roads and at the airports, since projected volumes remain near record levels."

Here is how AAA expects people to get there:

| How people travel | 2026 projection | Change vs. 2025 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| By car | 72.9 million | Down from 73.1 million |
| Domestic flights | 5.96 million | Down 0.2% |
| Buses, trains and cruises | 2.6 million | Up 4.1% |
| **Total** | **81.5 million** | **Down 0.1%** |

Cost is a big part of the story. AAA says August, September and October each set new monthly records at the pump, and November could too. The national average on Thanksgiving Day has never topped $4 a gallon; the highest so far was $3.58 in 2022. On the air side, AAA's booking data shows roundtrip domestic flights are 17% more expensive than last year, averaging $800 per ticket. The travel-booking company Hopper estimated in late October that Thanksgiving roundtrip domestic fares were up more than one-third, according to the [Associated Press report in the Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2026-11-24/thanksgiving-travel-could-be-messy-with-storms-coming-after-week-filled-with-airline-glitches).

If you are still shopping for gifts or groceries before the trip, our guide to [Black Friday 2026 sale dates](/blog/black-friday-2026-sale-dates-home-depot-lowes-walmart-target) has the store-by-store timing.

## The Thanksgiving travel weather forecast, day by day

The big weather story is a coastal storm that AccuWeather's Dan DePodwin described as a "potential nor'easter" that "comes at a time when millions of people are traveling and businesses are preparing for the holiday season." The West Coast has its own problems with rain, wind and unusually high tides.

This is the day-by-day picture from [The Weather Channel](https://weather.com/2026/11/24/holiday/thanksgiving/thanksgiving-travel-weather-forecast-us-storms):

- **Wednesday, November 25:** Locally heavy rain across much of the Southeast, from Florida and Georgia to the Carolinas, Alabama, Mississippi and Tennessee, moving into the mid-Atlantic and Ohio Valley by Wednesday night. Flight delays are possible in Atlanta. Rain may change to wet snow in eastern Missouri, western Illinois and southeast Iowa, with significant disruptions possible in St. Louis. Washington and western Oregon get rain and mountain snow.
- **Thanksgiving Day, November 26:** Heavy rain and strong gusts across the mid-Atlantic and Northeast. Expect significant flight delays at all Northeast hub airports plus St. Louis, with delays also possible at O'Hare, Pittsburgh, Cincinnati and Detroit. Interior northern New England could start with freezing rain and icy bridges.
- **Friday, November 27:** The storm's leftovers bring wind, rain and some wet snow to the Northeast and Ohio Valley, so expect delays there. The Pacific Northwest stays wet.
- **Saturday, November 28:** A decent travel day for most, with lingering showers in the far Northeast and snow in the northern and central Rockies.
- **Sunday, November 29:** Showers and a few thunderstorms in parts of the South and Midwest could slow O'Hare, and a new Pacific storm could cause delays out of San Francisco.

### East Coast: rain, wind and coastal flooding

The Journal News in New York reported that the Northeast could see 1 to 2 inches of rain, with coastal gusts of 50 mph or more and gusts over 40 mph possible farther inland along the Interstate 95 corridor. Strong onshore winds could add coastal flooding, and gusty winds could cause scattered power outages.

"A significant deterioration in the weather is expected starting Tuesday night across the Southeast and then spreading northward along and east of the Appalachians Wednesday through Friday," DePodwin said.

### West Coast: king tides and a San Francisco watch

The AP reported that moderate to major coastal flooding is forecast from Washington state to Southern California because of possibly record-breaking king tides, and San Francisco is bracing for flooding along its waterfront through Thursday.

## Why Thanksgiving flights feel shakier this year

The weather is landing on an air travel system that has had a rough November. According to the AP, an FAA network malfunction set off the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years. Within the past week, both United and Delta had their own outages, leading to hundreds of delays. Delta said more than 1,000 of its flights were delayed on Sunday, November 22, after an automated system that calculates aircraft weight malfunctioned. The airline said operations were stable on Monday.

The FAA expects to handle more than 417,000 flights over the nine-day holiday period, including more than 52,000 on Tuesday, November 24, [FOX23 and The National News Desk reported](https://fox23.com/news/nation-world/faa-federal-aviation-administration-forecasts-busiest-thanksgiving-air-travel-in-16-years-transportation-department-president-trump-tiger-team-secretary-sean-duffy-delta-united-ground-stop-storms-weather-forecast). On Monday the Trump administration announced an interagency group called the "Transportation Technology Transformation Tiger Team" to address concerns about air traffic control disruptions.

"As millions of Americans prepare to hit the road or take to the skies to spend the holiday season with their loved ones, the Trump Administration's message is clear: your safety is our number one priority," Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said.

Travelers at the airports were bracing for crowds anyway. "Thanksgiving is going to be nuts," Herbert Tobias, 32, told the AP at Newark Liberty International Airport. "I would literally recommend being here, they say three hours, take the three hours, you're going to need it."

Drivers heading out on Wednesday evening in the Southeast or mid-Atlantic, or returning on Friday in the Northeast and Ohio Valley, should watch for water on poorly drained roads and strong crosswinds.

## Airline waivers and your refund rights

Several airlines have already moved ahead of the weather. [ABC News reported](https://abcnews.com/GMA/Travel/airlines-issue-waivers-san-francisco-travelers-thanksgiving/story?id=138410676) that United, American and Southwest issued travel waivers for customers flying in and out of San Francisco International Airport on Wednesday. With United and American, the rebooked flight must depart by November 28. Southwest gives customers up to 14 days from the original departure date to reschedule. Waivers can expand quickly as a storm approaches, so check your airline's travel alerts page before you leave.

Here is what Department of Transportation rules say, as summarized by ABC News:

- **Canceled flight:** You are entitled to a refund if the airline cancels your flight, regardless of the reason, and you choose not to travel.
- **Significant delay:** Defined as three hours or more for a domestic flight and six hours or more for an international flight. If you accept the delayed or alternative flight, you are not entitled to a refund.
- **How you get paid:** Refunds must be automatic, prompt, in the original form of payment and for the full amount.
- **Delayed bags:** If a checked bag on a domestic flight is delayed more than 12 hours, or 15 to 30 hours on international flights, you can get your bag fee back.

Travelers also have the option to accept alternative arrangements or travel credits instead of a refund, ABC News noted. If you are stuck, keep your receipts and check any travel insurance you bought.

## Will the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade balloons fly?

This year's parade is the 100th, and wind is the wild card. City law prohibits Macy's from flying full-size balloons if sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts top 35 mph, according to the AP. The weather service expected the rain in New York to hold off until after the parade ends, but wind could still be an issue.

The Journal News noted that even if rain stays away for part of the morning, strong winds could complicate the balloons, and any shift in the storm's speed or track could change conditions on Thursday morning.

## What to watch next for Thanksgiving travel

- **Wednesday night:** How fast the storm moves north from the Carolinas. A faster storm means more trouble for late Wednesday drivers and flyers.
- **Thursday morning in New York:** Wind readings near the parade route and any balloon decision.
- **Friday and the weekend:** Recovery at Northeast hubs after Thursday's delays, plus the next Pacific storm that could slow San Francisco on Sunday.
- **Airline systems:** Any repeat of the United and Delta outages would land on the busiest days of the year. Check flight status before you leave home.

"I think weather won't be a significant factor except on Thanksgiving Day itself, and if there are any lingering impacts into Friday morning perhaps from delays that happen on Thanksgiving Day," Joe Wegman told the AP.
