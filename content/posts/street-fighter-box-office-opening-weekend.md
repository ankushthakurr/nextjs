> **The short version:** The Street Fighter box office debut is heading for roughly $31 million domestically, enough to win a crowded October weekend. That is a franchise best by a wide margin, but it lands below the studio's hopes and about 19% behind *Mortal Kombat II*. With an $82 million net budget, mixed reviews and a B- CinemaScore, overseas ticket sales will decide whether it becomes a hit.

The Street Fighter box office story this weekend is a win with an asterisk. The live-action Capcom adaptation, directed by Kitao Sakurai, is projected to take about $31 million from 3,575 theaters in the US and Canada, which puts it comfortably at No. 1. It also arrives on the softer side of what Paramount wanted, after the studio had been expecting a debut in the mid-$30 millions, [according to The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/street-fighter-box-office-you-can-see-everything-whalefall-1236734312/). Here is how the numbers break down, how the film compares with other video game movies, and what to watch as the actual totals come in.

## Street Fighter box office by the numbers

All figures below are weekend estimates published on Saturday, October 17, 2026. Studios report final Sunday estimates and Monday actuals, so expect the totals to move by a few hundred thousand dollars either way.

| Projected 3-day domestic opening | About $31 million | Deadline, THR, TheWrap |
| Theaters | 3,575 | Deadline, THR |
| Friday gross (including previews) | $12.5 million to $13 million | TheWrap, Deadline |
| Thursday previews | $4 million | TheWrap |

Outlets differ slightly on Friday. Deadline and The Hollywood Reporter put the day at $13 million, while TheWrap reported $12.5 million, and [Variety cited rival estimates](https://variety.com/2026/film/box-office/box-office-street-fighter-you-can-see-everything-whalefall-1236914827/) near $12.45 million. All of them agree on the roughly $31 million weekend.

The critics score also shifted as more reviews came in. Rotten Tomatoes' own first-reviews roundup listed 40%, The Hollywood Reporter cited 42%, and Deadline cited 47%. The safest summary is that critics land in the 40s.

## How the opening compares with other video game movies

The most direct comparison is *Mortal Kombat II*, the R-rated fighting game sequel Warner Bros. and New Line released in May 2026. It opened to $38.5 million, so *Street Fighter* is running about 19% behind it, [per Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/10/box-office-street-fighter-you-can-see-everything-1237159320/). Its Thursday previews also trailed the $5.2 million *Mortal Kombat II* made on its first night.

There are brighter comparisons too. The $4 million preview figure is ahead of the $3 million that Paramount's first *Sonic the Hedgehog* made in previews in early 2020, a film that went on to a $58 million three-day start. And against its own franchise history, the new film is in another league.

- **Street Fighter (1994):** The Universal release starring Jean-Claude Van Damme opened to $6.8 million and finished with $33.4 million domestically, unadjusted for inflation. The 2026 film's opening weekend alone nearly matches that entire run.
- **Mortal Kombat II (2026):** $38.5 million opening, B CinemaScore, 86% Rotten Tomatoes audience score.
- **Street Fighter (2026):** About $31 million opening, B- CinemaScore, about 80% audience score.

Interestingly, TheWrap noted that the 1994 film also earned a B- CinemaScore, so audiences have given both versions the same grade more than three decades apart.

## Who went to see it

Deadline's breakdown of PostTrak exit polling shows a notably diverse opening crowd:

- Latino and Hispanic moviegoers: 30%
- Caucasian moviegoers: 29%
- Black moviegoers: 21%
- Asian American moviegoers: 14%
- Other and Native American moviegoers: 6%

Families were a small slice. Parents and kids under 12 made up only 11% of the business, which matters for the long run because family audiences tend to keep films playing for weeks. PostTrak's "definite recommend" figure, which tracks how many viewers would actively tell friends to go, came in at 65% overall and 60% among kids under 12. Deadline described that as OK rather than strong.

Online buzz was not the problem. Social media monitoring firm RelishMix counted a combined reach of 920.6 million across TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook and X heading into the weekend. That is larger than the figures it recorded for *A Minecraft Movie* (772.4 million), *Five Nights at Freddy's* (488.5 million) and *Mortal Kombat II* (452.6 million). The gap between huge awareness and a solid but not spectacular opening suggests plenty of people watched the trailers without buying a ticket on day one.

## What critics are saying about Street Fighter

Reviews have been split, and the split follows a clear line: fans of the games tend to enjoy the fidelity, while general critics find the tone scattered. Rotten Tomatoes summed up the [first wave of reviews](https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/street-fighter-2026-first-reviews/) as praising the Easter eggs, animated sequences and lively action, while calling the film tonally messy, a little juvenile and "faithful to a fault."

Some of the more positive takes:

- "In so many respects, *Street Fighter* works exactly the way you want a big screen adaptation of *Street Fighter* to work." (Liz Shannon Miller, Consequence)
- "It's as much an adaptation of the Capcom fighting game as it is of the vibe of a '90s mall arcade." (Matt Singer, ScreenCrush)
- "The look and overall vibe of the movie captures that of the games and there are plenty of Easter Eggs, one-liners, and references to keep fans entertained." (Tessa Smith, Mama's Geeky)

And the more critical ones:

- The Hollywood Reporter's Frank Scheck called it a "jumbled tonal mess" that is "only sporadically entertaining."
- "It doesn't capture the core of its source material." (Trace Sauveur, Awards Watch)
- TheWrap's William Bibbiani split the difference: "Kitao Sakurai made every possible *Street Fighter* movie at the exact same time. For better and for worse, he succeeded."

According to the Rotten Tomatoes roundup, the story builds through a tournament and flashbacks toward a showdown with M. Bison and ends on an unresolved cliffhanger, a clear signal that the studios hope to make more.

## Can Street Fighter turn a profit?

The short answer is that it depends on the rest of the world. The roughly $82 million net budget, co-financed by Legendary and Capcom according to Deadline, does not include marketing costs, and a $31 million domestic start will not cover that on its own. TheWrap noted that overseas grosses will be important for this co-production, and international numbers had not been widely reported at the time of writing.

Here is how the case looks from what is known so far:

- **Working for it:** a No. 1 finish, a franchise-best start, record-level social media reach and a younger, diverse opening crowd.
- **Working against it:** critics' scores in the 40s, a B- CinemaScore, a "definite recommend" score Deadline called only OK, and a small family audience.
- **Still unknown:** international and worldwide totals, and how much the film drops in its second weekend.

## What to watch next

- **Sunday estimates and Monday actuals.** These will confirm whether the opening lands at, above or below the $31 million projection.
- **International and worldwide totals.** Overseas results are the biggest swing factor for profitability.
- **The second-weekend drop.** A fall of 60% or more would point to front-loaded fan demand; a smaller drop would suggest word of mouth is holding up.
- **Sequel signals.** The cliffhanger ending leaves the door open, but any follow-up will likely depend on the worldwide total.
