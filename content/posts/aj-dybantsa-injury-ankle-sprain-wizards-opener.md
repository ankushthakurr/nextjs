> **The short version:** The AJ Dybantsa injury is a left ankle sprain suffered in the Wizards' 107-106 preseason loss to New Orleans on Friday night. Coach Brian Keefe says he was pulled as a precaution. Washington opens the regular season against Milwaukee on Wednesday, October 21, and his availability is the biggest open question of the Wizards' week.

The AJ Dybantsa injury landed at the worst possible moment for Washington. Five days before Washington's first debut by a No. 1 overall pick since John Wall in 2010, the 19-year-old rolled his left ankle in the final tune-up of the preseason and did not return. Here is everything confirmed so far, what a sprain like this usually means on the calendar, and what to watch between now and opening night.

## What happened to AJ Dybantsa on Friday night

Dybantsa went up for a mid-range jumper in the second quarter against the [New Orleans Pelicans](https://basketnews.com/news-257518-aj-dybantsa-injury-latest-updates-as-no-1-pick-exits-wizards-preseason-finale.html), landed awkwardly, and was slow to get up. He was helped off the floor and walked to the locker room, where cameras caught him mouthing "I'm good" on the way down the tunnel, [according to Heavy](https://heavy.com/sports/nba/washington-wizards/aj-dybantsa-debut-in-doubt-after-injury/).

The Wizards' TV broadcast then announced he would not return for "precautionary reasons." Second-year guard Tre Johnson started the third quarter in his place.

Before leaving, Dybantsa was having an efficient night: **12 points, three rebounds and a block in 15 minutes**. Washington went on to lose 107-106, finishing an exhibition slate that otherwise gave fans plenty of reasons for optimism.

## What the Wizards are saying about the ankle sprain

Head coach Brian Keefe kept his postgame update brief and deliberately calm:

> "AJ's got an ankle sprain. We just took him out of the game for precaution. We'll look at it more when we get more information."

That wording matters. "Precaution" in a meaningless preseason game is standard practice for a franchise cornerstone, and the team did not mention any structural concern or follow-up imaging in its first comments. [BasketNews reported](https://basketnews.com/news-257524-aj-dybantsa-injury-update-wizards-coach-gives-new-details.html) the sprain as mild and said Washington hopes he will be ready for opening night.

What we **don't** know yet:

- Whether the team ordered an MRI, and if so, what it showed
- Whether it is a common lateral (low) sprain or a high ankle sprain, which typically takes much longer
- How the ankle responds to the first 48 to 72 hours of treatment, which is usually when teams get real clarity

Until the Wizards release an official injury report ahead of Wednesday, any timeline is an educated estimate. We'll update this story when that report lands.

## How long do mild ankle sprains usually take?

Ankle sprains are graded by how much the ligaments are stretched or torn. According to [Mass General Brigham](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/conditions/785/sprains-ankle), recovery generally looks like this:

| Sprain grade | What it means | Typical recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grade 1 (mild) | Ligament stretched, minimal tearing | About 1 to 3 weeks |
| Grade 2 (moderate) | Partial ligament tear | About 4 to 6 weeks |
| Grade 3 (severe) | Complete tear | Several months |

If the early "mild" characterization holds, Dybantsa falls in the first row. NBA players have round-the-clock treatment, so they tend to recover at the quick end of a range like that. Still, five days is tight even for a grade 1 sprain, and teams are notoriously conservative with 19-year-olds on long rookie contracts. A short absence would not be a surprise, and it would not be a sign that anything is seriously wrong.

## Will Dybantsa play in the season opener vs. the Bucks?

Washington's regular season opens **Wednesday, October 21, against the Milwaukee Bucks**, followed by a game against the Toronto Raptors on Friday, October 23.

Three realistic scenarios:

1. **He plays Wednesday, possibly on a minutes limit.** This is the outcome the team is hoping for, and it is plausible if the swelling settles quickly and he moves well in practice early next week.
2. **He sits the opener and returns against Toronto or the following week.** This is the classic "one or two games for caution" path that teams often choose with young stars and minor sprains.
3. **A longer absence of two weeks or more.** This would usually follow imaging that reveals more than a mild sprain, or a high ankle sprain diagnosis. Nothing reported so far points this way.

The practical tell will be practice participation. If Dybantsa is a full participant by Monday or Tuesday, scenario one becomes the favorite. If he is listed as questionable and limited to shooting, scenario two is more likely.

## Why the AJ Dybantsa injury matters so much in Washington

It is hard to overstate how central Dybantsa is to the Wizards' new direction. Washington finished last season 17-65, has not made the playoffs since 2020-21, and has not posted a winning record since 2017-18, [per NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/wizards-take-aj-dybantsa-no-1-2026-draft). Winning the lottery and landing the No. 1 pick for the first time since 2010 changed the mood around the franchise overnight.

### A historic college season

The 6-foot-9 wing arrived as the top-ranked high school player in his class and spent one season at BYU, where he:

- **Led the nation in scoring** at 25.5 points per game
- Scored 43 points against rival Utah and 40 against Kansas State in the Big 12 Tournament
- Put up **93 points in three Big 12 Tournament games**, breaking Kevin Durant's conference tournament scoring record

He became the first BYU player ever drafted No. 1 overall, going ahead of Kansas guard Darryn Peterson (No. 2, Utah) and Duke forward Cameron Boozer (No. 3, Memphis).

### A promising preseason

Dybantsa looked comfortable right away. Across his first four preseason games he averaged:

| Stat | Per game |
| --- | --- |
| Points | 12.75 |
| Rebounds | 4.25 |
| Assists | 1.75 |
| Steals | 1.5 |
| Minutes | 21.5 |

Those are efficient numbers for a teenager playing limited minutes next to veterans, and the steals hint at defensive instincts that weren't the headline of his scouting report.

## A deeper roster softens the blow

Unlike many No. 1 picks, Dybantsa does not have to carry a bad team by himself, and he has said as much. Washington's veteran core now includes Trae Young, Anthony Davis, Khris Middleton and DeAndre Ayton.

"I feel like there's not a lot of pressure on me because I have such a great team around me," Dybantsa said in comments [reported by Heavy](https://heavy.com/sports/nba/washington-wizards/aj-dybantsa-debut-in-doubt-after-injury/). "I don't have to come in and carry this huge load."

That depth is exactly why the Wizards can afford patience. If Dybantsa needs an extra game or two, Keefe can lean on his veterans and on young wings like Johnson rather than rushing his most important long-term asset back for a single October game.
