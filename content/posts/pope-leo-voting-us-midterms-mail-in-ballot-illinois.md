> **The short version:** Pope Leo voting in the U.S. midterms is now confirmed. The Vatican said on October 30 that the Chicago-born pontiff sent in a mail-in ballot for the November 3 election, making him the first pope to vote in an American election. He did it the same way any American living overseas can, and the Vatican has not said whom he picked.

Pope Leo voting in an American election sounds like a trivia question, but as of this week it is a fact. On Friday, October 30, 2026, the Holy See Press Office confirmed that Pope Leo XIV, born Robert Francis Prevost, had submitted a mail-in ballot for the U.S. midterm elections. News outlets from [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/30/europe/pope-leo-voting-us-midterms-intl) to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/31/pope-leo-becomes-first-pontiff-to-vote-in-us-general-election) called it a first for any pope, and searches for "did Pope Leo vote" spiked overnight.

This guide covers what the Vatican actually said, where the pope is registered, what his past voting record shows, what races are on his ballot, and why a sitting head of state can still vote as a private American citizen.

## What the Vatican said about Pope Leo voting

The confirmation came in a short statement from the Holy See Press Office, reported by [Vatican News](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/pope/news/2026-10/pope-leo-xiv-votes-usa-midterm-elections.html). The key lines:

- "Pope Leo XIV has exercised his right to vote as a United States citizen, whenever he was eligible to do so."
- "In keeping with this long-standing practice of civic participation, he has submitted his mail-in ballot in Chicago."
- "The Holy Father has also confirmed that, during his years of residence in Peru he exercised his right to vote whenever he was eligible to do so."
- "The Holy Father expresses his belief in the right and the duty of citizens to participate in democratic processes."

The statement did not name any candidate or party, and none of the outlets that covered it reported how he voted. Al Jazeera noted plainly that "the Vatican did not say which candidates Leo voted for."

The framing matters. The Vatican presented the vote as a continuation of a personal habit, not a political statement, and paired it with a general endorsement of civic participation. The U.S. Conference of Catholic Bishops echoed that tone. Chieko Noguchi, its director of public affairs, told Al Jazeera: "In voting, our Holy Father sets the example that encourages each citizen to exercise their civic responsibility." The bishops' conference also urged Catholics to put "our faith before our politics" and to "uphold human dignity and the common good."

## Is this really the first time a pope has voted?

Yes, as far as anyone covering the story can tell. Leo is the first American pope, elected in May 2025, so no earlier pontiff had a U.S. vote to cast. NBC News, CNN and Al Jazeera all describe him as the first pope to vote in a U.S. election.

CNN went a step further, reporting that he is "likely the first to vote in a secular democratic election" of any kind. British historian and author Michael Walsh told CNN: "I don't think popes have ever voted in a democratic election before."

What makes it possible is citizenship. Leo kept his U.S. citizenship after moving abroad and, according to the Vatican, kept voting whenever he was eligible, including during his years in Peru.

## Where Pope Leo is registered and how his ballot works

The details here are a little tangled, because different outlets describe the same ballot in slightly different ways.

- **The Vatican** says he "submitted his mail-in ballot in Chicago," the city where he was born.
- **CNN** reports he is registered as Robert Francis Prevost in **New Lenox**, a Chicago suburb where his brother John lives.
- **NBC News** reports the ballot request was confirmed by Kim Fladhammer, chief of staff at the **Will County Clerk's Office**, and that the ballot's journey runs from Vatican City to Joliet, the county seat.

These accounts fit together: New Lenox sits in Will County, in the greater Chicago area. The Vatican's "in Chicago" reads as shorthand for his home region rather than a Chicago city address.

According to Al Jazeera, Leo requested the ballot on October 3. CNN reports it is not clear whether the ballot was mailed directly from the Vatican. His brother John visited the Vatican on Wednesday, October 28, with pilgrims from St. Jude's Parish in New Lenox, CNN said. Before the Vatican statement, a Will County clerk's official told the Chicago Sun-Times that Robert Francis Prevost was on a "list of individuals who requested a vote by mail ballot for the upcoming election," according to CNN.

Al Jazeera also reported that the request came 10 days before the pope had surgery to remove a cancerous tumor from his right lung. He left the hospital on October 17 and has been gradually resuming his schedule.

## Pope Leo's voting record, by the numbers

Public election records give a rough outline of how often Prevost has voted, though not how he voted. Illinois primary records show which party's ballot a voter asked for, not which candidates they chose.

| Election type | Years on record | What it shows | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General elections | 2012, 2014, 2018, 2024 | Cast ballots, including the 2024 presidential election | NBC News, CNN |
| Democratic primaries | 2008, 2010 | Requested Democratic ballots while registered in Cook County | Al Jazeera |
| Republican primaries | 2012, 2014, 2016 | Requested Republican ballots after moving to Will County | NBC News, Al Jazeera |

Al Jazeera, citing the Illinois State Board of Elections, counts five primaries since 2000: two Democratic and three Republican. NBC News notes that Illinois voters do not register by party and can pick a party's primary ballot each cycle, which is why these records say little about his personal politics.

The mix of Democratic and Republican primary ballots has been widely noted because it resists easy labels. It lines up with his move from heavily Democratic Cook County to more conservative Will County, as Al Jazeera described.

## What is on Pope Leo's ballot

Illinois is a solidly Democratic state, and the races on the pope's ballot reflect that. According to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/pope-leo-requested-absentee-ballot-illinois-vote-midterms-rcna606468), his ballot includes:

- **Governor:** Gov. JB Pritzker, running for re-election.
- **U.S. Senate:** an open seat, with Democrat Juliana Stratton seeking to succeed retiring Sen. Dick Durbin.
- **U.S. House:** Rep. Jonathan Jackson, a Democrat, seeking re-election.
- **State and county races**, as on every Illinois midterm ballot, according to CNN.

There is some history between the pope and the governor. CNN reports that Leo met Pritzker at the Vatican in November 2025, where they discussed immigration enforcement, and that the pope later expressed disapproval of Illinois' medically assisted death law, which Pritzker signed. CNN also notes that in 2011, then-Rev. Robert Prevost messaged Illinois Gov. Pat Quinn to thank him for abolishing the death penalty in the state.

Nationally, the stakes are much bigger than one Illinois ballot. Vatican News summarized the midterms as a contest for all 435 House seats, 35 of the Senate's 100 seats and governorships in 36 states. ## Can a pope legally vote in US elections?

Yes. Vatican News points out that eligible U.S. citizens living abroad, along with military members stationed away from home, can cast absentee ballots in federal elections. Al Jazeera adds that U.S. federal law allows citizens living abroad to vote absentee even if they work for foreign governments.

The pope's case is unusual because he is not just working for a foreign government. He is the head of one. According to Al Jazeera, Leo holds three citizenships: American, Peruvian (he lived in Peru as a priest and bishop for more than 20 years) and Vatican. Holding a foreign office does not automatically cost an American their citizenship, but the U.S. State Department says on its website that it may "actively review" the status of Americans who "serve as a foreign head of state, foreign head of government, or foreign minister," Al Jazeera reported.

CNN summed it up simply: even though he is a head of state, the pope remains a U.S. citizen and is permitted to vote in U.S. elections.

## The Trump backdrop

The vote lands after a public and unusually sharp relationship between the pope and the White House.

- NBC News reports Leo has criticized the U.S. war against Iran and the administration's treatment of immigrants. The two traded words in April after Leo called for peace in the war that began with U.S.-Israeli strikes on February 28.
- Al Jazeera reports that earlier this year President Donald Trump called Leo "weak" and captive to the "radical left." NBC News quotes Trump saying, "I'm not a big fan of Pope Leo."
- Leo has said, according to Al Jazeera: "I'm not afraid of the Trump administration or of speaking out loudly about the message of the Gospel, which is what the church works for."
- Vice President JD Vance, who is Catholic, has also criticized the pope over his comments on U.S. foreign policy, NBC News reports.
- CNN notes Trump has been less critical lately and often mentions that the pope's eldest brother, Louis, supports him.

None of that tells us how the pope voted, and the Vatican has been careful not to suggest it does. What it does explain is why a routine absentee ballot turned into a trending search on Google in the United States. More world coverage is in our [world news section](/category/world).
