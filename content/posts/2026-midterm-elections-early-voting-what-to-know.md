> **The short version:** The 2026 midterm elections are on Tuesday, November 3, and control of both chambers of Congress is on the line. Republicans hold narrow majorities today, more than 11 million Americans had voted by October 20, and the rules for registering, voting early and returning a mail ballot differ a lot from state to state. Here is what to know in the final nine days.

The 2026 midterm elections are now nine days away, and searches for "when is election day", "early voting near me" and "do I need ID to vote" are climbing fast. With early voting open in most states and record sums flowing into advertising, this guide pulls together what is actually at stake, what the early numbers show, and the practical deadlines that decide whether your ballot counts.

## What is on the ballot in the 2026 midterm elections

Midterms happen halfway through a presidential term, so the president is not on the ballot. Almost everything else is. According to [U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2026-10-20/2026-midterm-elections-voter-registration-deadlines-and-early-voting-dates-by-state), voters will fill:

- **All 435 seats** in the U.S. House of Representatives
- **33 U.S. Senate seats**
- **39 governorships** (Reuters counts governor races in 36 states; the difference reflects how territories and special cases are counted)
- Thousands of state legislative seats, judges, county offices, and local bonds and ballot measures

[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/what-you-need-know-about-2026-us-midterm-elections-2026-10-19/) notes that legislative seats are up in nearly every state, which matters because state lawmakers write election rules and, in many states, draw congressional maps.

### Who controls Congress right now

| Chamber | Current split | What flips it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| House | Republicans 218, Democrats 214 (two vacancies, one independent caucusing with Republicans) | A net gain of a handful of seats |
| Senate | Republicans 53, Democrats 47 | A net Democratic gain of four seats |

*Source: Reuters, October 19, 2026.*

Those margins are thin. A shift of just a few House districts would hand the chamber to the other party, and with it control of committees, investigations and the legislative agenda for the final two years of President Donald Trump's term.

## The Senate races everyone is watching

Reuters lists the key Senate battlegrounds as **Texas, Ohio, Michigan, Maine, North Carolina, Iowa and Georgia**. Advertising follows the stakes: more than $3.8 billion had been spent on aired political ads by October 12, a record, with the heaviest spending in the Texas, Ohio and Michigan Senate races, according to AdImpact data cited by Reuters. Reuters also reports Elon Musk has spent about $366 million supporting Republicans.

In Texas, NBC DFW reports the Senate race is between Attorney General Ken Paxton and state Rep. James Talarico, and Governor Greg Abbott faces Hinojosa in the governor race. Down the ballot, the Houston area alone will send several new members to Congress. ABC13 reports that Rep. Dan Crenshaw lost his Republican primary to Steve Toth, and that redistricting pushed Rep. Al Green out of his district.

### What the polls and enthusiasm data say

Polls are snapshots, not predictions, but they shape the conversation:

- A September Reuters/Ipsos poll put approval of President Trump's job performance at 32%, which Reuters describes as an all-time low.
- Reuters also reports evidence that Democrats are more enthusiastic about voting this year than Republicans.
- Early voting data shows a partisan gap. U.S. News, citing NBC News, reports that roughly 51% of registered Democrats had voted early as of October 20, compared with 32% of registered Republicans.

Early vote shares do not tell you who will win. Republicans have tended to vote in larger numbers on Election Day itself in recent cycles, so early leads can shrink once in-person ballots are counted.

## Early voting 2026: the numbers so far

According to the University of Florida Election Lab tracker cited by U.S. News, **more than 11 million people had already voted by October 20**:

- More than 3 million in-person early votes
- More than 7 million mail ballots

If turnout resembles 2022, more than 100 million Americans will cast a ballot in total, Reuters estimates.

U.S. News reports that 47 states and Washington, D.C. offer early voting. Alabama, Mississippi and New Hampshire do not offer no-excuse early in-person voting. Early in-person ballots are usually scanned when cast, but results are not tallied until polls close on Election Day.

### Sample early voting windows

| State or city | Early voting window | Election Day hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Texas | Oct. 19 to Oct. 30 | 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. |
| Arkansas | Oct. 19 to Nov. 2 (ends 5 p.m.) | Set by state law |
| Indiana | Started Oct. 6, 28 days total | 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. |
| Chicago | Ward sites from Oct. 19 through Nov. 2 | Supersite open 6 a.m. to 7 p.m. |
| California | Registration still possible Oct. 24 to Nov. 2 and on Election Day | Set by state law |

*Sources: NBC DFW, Arkansas Advocate, ACLU of Indiana, Block Club Chicago, U.S. News.*

Always confirm hours with your county election office, because local sites can differ from statewide rules.

## Registration, ID and mail ballot deadlines

The single biggest reason ballots are rejected is a missed deadline. Here is what the sources say:

**Registration.** U.S. News reports that 24 states and Washington, D.C. allow same-day registration during early voting, on Election Day, or both, and 17 of those allow it throughout early voting and on Election Day. Chicago, for example, offers same-day registration at every early voting site, according to Block Club Chicago. But regular registration deadlines have already passed in states including Alabama, Florida, Mississippi, Ohio, South Carolina, Texas and West Virginia.

**ID.** Rules vary widely. Texas asks for one of several approved forms of photo ID, according to ABC13 and NBC DFW. The [Arkansas Advocate](https://arkansasadvocate.com/2026/10/19/arkansas-explained-how-to-vote-early-in-the-2026-midterm-election/) confirms Arkansas requires ID. North Dakota does not have voter registration at all, only a valid state ID at the polls, per U.S. News. In Indiana, the ACLU notes that regular voter ID rules apply and voters do not need a birth certificate or citizenship papers to vote.

**Mail ballots.** U.S. News says completed ballots generally must be postmarked on or before Election Day, but each state sets its own receipt deadline for counting. Texas's last day to apply for a mail ballot was October 23, according to NBC DFW. If you still have a mail ballot at home, many states let you drop it at an official drop box or election office instead of mailing it this close to the deadline.

### Your rights at the polls

The ACLU of Indiana's guide lists protections that apply in most states:

- If you are in line when polls close, you can still vote.
- If there is a problem with your registration, you can ask for a provisional ballot.
- If you make a mistake, you can ask for a replacement ballot before you submit it.
- You can ask for help or accommodations, including language assistance where required.

## When will we know the results?

Results begin coming in after polls close in each time zone on November 3. Close races, especially in states that count mail ballots that arrive after Election Day, can take days to call. Watch for:

1. **Early returns versus final counts.** States process mail and in-person ballots in different orders, so leads can swing overnight.
2. **House control.** With a 218-214 split, control may not be clear on election night if several close districts remain uncounted.
3. **Runoffs.** Some states, including Georgia, require runoffs when no candidate passes a vote threshold, which can extend the Senate picture into December.

## How to make a voting plan in five minutes

1. **Check your registration** on your state election office website, and look up your polling place, since it can change between elections.
2. **Pick a time.** Early voting lines are usually shorter on weekday mornings.
3. **Bring the right ID** for your state.
4. **Return your mail ballot early** or use a drop box.
5. **Read your whole ballot.** Local measures, like Houston's Proposition A on marijuana enforcement and no-knock warrants reported by ABC13, often get decided by small margins.
